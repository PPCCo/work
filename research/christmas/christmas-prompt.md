Research method, evidence quality and scoring
The research combines (a) Etsy’s official trend and seller guidance, (b) public listing snapshots surfaced by current searches, (c) competitor offer/price/review clues where visible, and (d) explicit product hypotheses based on recurring buyer workflows. Listing pages can change and search results are personalised, so the public sample is directional rather than a census. Etsy itself advises sellers to use Marketplace Insights to compare buyer search terms and the number of listings available for those terms. [S2, S6]
Term	Meaning
Observed fact	A public listing title, displayed price, page count, review count, sales count or current Etsy trend statistic captured in the source page.
Adjacent-market evidence	A listing exists in the parent category (e.g., a generic pet sitter form), but the proposed micro-niche is narrower (e.g., foster-to-adoption continuity).
White-space hypothesis	A specific buyer/workflow combination looked less directly saturated in a limited public search. This does not mean zero listings or proven demand.
Demand score (1–5)	Qualitative strength of observable trend/parent-category evidence, not a keyword-volume estimate.
Competition estimate (1–5)	Relative saturation estimate from a limited public sample. 1 means lower observed exact-match saturation; 5 means crowded. Not listing count.
