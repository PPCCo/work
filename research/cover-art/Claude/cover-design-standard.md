# Cover Design Standard

The craft baseline every cover must meet. This is what separates a *designed* cover from a *generated picture*. Read it before designing any cover.

---

## 1. The three tests

Every cover must pass all three before it ships.

1. **Thumbnail test.** At 160 px wide (a marketplace search thumbnail) the title is still readable and the main subject still reads as one clear shape.
2. **Squint test.** Blur your eyes: you should see 2–3 big value masses (dark / mid / light), with the focal point as the highest-contrast spot on the page.
3. **Three-second promise.** A stranger can say what *kind* of book it is, *who* it is for, and what *mood* it has within three seconds.

---

## 2. One idea, one focal point, one hierarchy

- **One big idea.** State it as a single sentence, the *cover promise* ("a sleepy fox carries a lantern through a snowy forest"). If you need "and" twice, it is two ideas; pick one.
- **One focal point.** A single area carries the highest contrast, sharpest detail and strongest color. Everything else supports it.
- **Reading order.** Level 1: title lockup + hero image (co-equal). Level 2: subtitle / series line. Level 3: publisher name. Optional: age badge or volume number if supplied in the brief.
- **Size ratio.** Title cap-height ≥ 3× the series line; series ≥ 1.5× the publisher. The publisher line is the smallest element and is allowed to disappear in the thumbnail.

---

## 3. Composition archetypes

Pick one per variant. Variants of the same book must use *different* archetypes.

| Archetype | Structure | Best for |
|---|---|---|
| **Iconic Hero** | One large subject with a bold silhouette, slightly off-center or cropped; calm field around it | Kids reading, family, puzzle, nonfiction |
| **Scene Window** | A full scene with depth; title sits in a quiet sky / ground band | Storybooks, anime, digital art, painting |
| **Frame & Emblem** | Subject inside a medallion, arch, wreath or ornamental frame; title above/below | Mandala, adult coloring, classic, gift-style |
| **Pattern Field** | Repeating motif fills the cover with a clear cut-out or panel for the title | Coloring, puzzle, educational |
| **Type-as-Image** | Oversized lettering is the main graphic; imagery lives inside, behind or around the letters | Adult nonfiction, teen, bold series branding |
| **Layered Depth** | Distinct foreground / mid / background planes, scale contrast, atmospheric perspective; subject overlaps the title zone slightly | Anime, concept art, adventure, nonfiction |
| **Split / Diagonal** | Cover divided by a diagonal or two-tone split; subject crosses the division | Teen, puzzle, nonfiction, modern |
| **Paper-Cut Collage** | Layered cut-paper or flat-shape shapes with soft shadows; tactile | Kids, educational, storybooks |
| **Minimal Poster** | One object, huge negative space, restrained palette | Adult nonfiction, photography, sketch, photorealism |
| **Gallery Plate** | Artwork presented as a framed plate on a toned, textured ground with generous margin | Painting, sketch, photography, art books |

---

## 4. Layout and safe zones (front cover, full bleed)

- Keep **all text ≥ 8 % from every edge** of the canvas. The artwork may bleed to the edge; critical elements may not.
- The subject may deliberately crop off an edge, but **never crop a face, hands or the title** awkwardly.
- **Title zone:** top third (default), lower third, or integrated into the scene. Whatever the choice, the title sits on a **quiet zone**: calmer, lower detail, strong contrast to the lettering.
- **Series line:** small, tracked-out line above the title, or a banner/ribbon below it.
- **Publisher line:** bottom centre, smallest text, ~4–6 % of canvas height, separated by a thin rule or generous space.
- **Balance:** prefer asymmetric balance (a large subject countered by a smaller dense element). Dead-centre symmetry is only for Frame & Emblem / Mandala.
- **Eye path:** the eye should travel title → subject → series → publisher without detours. Diagonals and subject gaze/pointing guide it.

---

## 5. Color

- **60 / 30 / 10:** 60 % dominant, 30 % secondary, 10 % accent. The accent goes on or near the focal point.
- **Maximum four hues plus neutrals.** More than that reads as noise unless the genre is deliberately rainbow (kids' coloring) and a hierarchy is still obvious.
- **Value before hue.** Establish three planes of value first (dark / mid / light). If it fails in greyscale it fails in color.
- **Harmony recipes** (pick and name one in the design spec):
  - Analogous + one complementary accent
  - Split-complementary
  - Soft triadic (desaturated secondaries)
  - Monochrome + one hot accent
  - Luminous on dark (glowing subject against near-black / deep navy / indigo)
  - Warm paper + ink + one spot color
- **Saturation by audience:**
  - Ages 3–6: high chroma, clean and simple.
  - Ages 6–9: vivid with depth and shading.
  - Ages 9–12: bold contrast, richer lighting.
  - Teens: stylized duotone/limited palettes, strong graphic contrast.
  - Adults: restrained, sophisticated, muted base with one deliberate accent. (Adult *coloring* and *mandala* covers may glow with jewel tones on dark.)
- **Title contrast:** the title color against its immediate background must reach ~4.5 : 1 minimum (aim for 7 : 1). Outlines or soft shadows may be used to rescue contrast.
- **Avoid by default:** teal-and-orange everywhere, purple-pink neon gradients, over-bloomed lens flare, muddy brown mid-tones, and rainbow without hierarchy. Use them only if the genre truly demands it.

---

## 6. Typography (rendered inside the image)

- **At most two lettering styles.** The title style carries the book's personality; secondary lines stay simple.
- **Describe lettering by traits, not font names:** weight, width, contrast, terminals, case, finish (see `qwen-prompt-craft.md` for the vocabulary).
- **Case:** capitals for short titles and strong geometry; title case for long titles or literary/elegant tone.
- **Break lines by meaning**, not by width. 2–4 lines. Shape the lockup (stacked, arched, ribbon) deliberately.
- **Effects:** at most two of: outline, soft drop shadow, emboss, foil/metallic, inline. Restraint reads as professional.
- **Length:** title ≤ 6 words preferred (≤ 8 max). If the real title is longer, the *subtitle* line carries the rest. Short text renders reliably.
- **Integration:** let image and title interact (subject overlaps a letter, shadow falls across the lettering, letters sit behind a foreground element), but only while the title stays fully legible.

---

## 7. Light, depth and material

- **One key light.** Add a rim light for separation. Shadows share a hue family (not neutral grey).
- **Three depth planes** (foreground / mid / background) with scale contrast and a touch of atmospheric perspective.
- **Tactile material:** paper grain, ink bleed, canvas weave, brush texture, enamel, foil. Material signals human craft and kills the "plastic AI skin" look.
- **Detail economy:** one hero area of high detail; everything else simplified. Detail everywhere = detail nowhere.

---

## 8. Escaping the bland, mechanical cover

If a draft shows any *smell* in the left column, apply a *cure* from the right.

| Smell | Cure |
|---|---|
| Subject centered and floating on a plain gradient | Give it an environment with depth, or an intentional graphic ground (pattern, paper, frame); shift the subject off-center; crop or overlap |
| Everything at the same scale | Introduce scale contrast: one large hero, small supporting elements |
| Flat, even lighting | Choose a light direction and time of day; add rim light and a cast shadow |
| Stock palette (generic blue/purple) | Name a palette with roles (60/30/10) and a deliberate accent |
| Title merely pasted over the picture | Build a quiet zone and a lettering lockup; let image and type interact |
| Empty background | Add a secondary storytelling element or a texture/pattern with low contrast |
| Clutter with no focal point | Cut elements; enforce one hero area; reduce background contrast |
| Subject stands facing camera, no story | Choose a *moment* (mid-action, discovery, glance) and a point of view (low angle, over-shoulder) |
| Glossy plastic skin, no material | Specify a medium and surface (gouache, paper-cut, ink and wash, oil impasto) |
| Symmetry by default | Use asymmetric balance unless the archetype requires symmetry |

---

## 9. Honest covers

- **The cover shows what is inside.** Coloring books show the line-art style (with a few colored samples to demonstrate possibility). Puzzle books show the puzzle motif. Art books show the art style.
- **No false claims:** no "bestseller", award seals, star ratings, price tags, or review quotes.
- **Age badge / volume number** only when supplied in the brief, and only as exact text.
- **No extra text.** Any grid, sign, label, book spine or screen in the artwork must be abstract with no legible characters, because the three declared text layers are the only text allowed.
