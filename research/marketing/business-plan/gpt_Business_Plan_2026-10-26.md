# Business Plan: Research-Led AI Publishing & Digital Products

**Prepared:** 26 October 2026  
**Business concept:** A research-led publishing business powered by an agentic AI publishing framework, combining direct-to-consumer digital products, print-on-demand publishing, and a productized B2B publishing service.  
**Initial operating assumption:** Founder-led, based in Australia, English-first, selling primarily into the US and other English-speaking markets. This is a planning assumption, not a restriction.

> **Decision in one paragraph:** Do not launch a huge catalogue across every format at once. Use the framework to find and validate narrow audience problems, then launch a small number of distinct product collections with unusually strong content, design, and QA. Start with (1) pet- and relationship-led personalised keepsakes, (2) seasonal/occasion printable experiences and art, (3) sharply differentiated educational/activity products with subject-matter review, and (4) a B2B “Niche Publishing Launch Sprint” service for sellers, educators, creators, and small publishers. Use Etsy and Amazon KDP for marketplace intent, Pinterest and short-form video for discovery, and a direct storefront/email list for ownership and repeat purchases. Expand only after observed customer behaviour—not keyword excitement alone—supports it.

---

## 1. Executive summary

The core asset is not the ability to make images or books cheaply. It is the **end-to-end system** that can research a niche, identify an audience, formulate a product promise, create and audit the material, package it for a platform, and use market signals to improve the next release.

The business should turn that asset into two complementary engines:

1. **Consumer publishing engine:** sell distinctive digital downloads, printable collections, illustrated books and selected print-on-demand titles. Build cohesive collections around a clear audience, occasion, interest, or problem rather than publishing disconnected one-off files.
2. **B2B publishing engine:** sell a defined outcome to people who want to publish but lack the time, production capability, design skills, research discipline, or capacity to test niches. Start as a productized service; consider a subscription or software offering only after repeated demand and workflow economics are demonstrated.

The B2B engine is likely to be the better early monetisation experiment because it can command a higher order value and directly addresses a costly production bottleneck. Consumer products create reusable intellectual property, proof of demand, and a long-term catalogue, but marketplace discovery and conversion are uncertain. These are strategic hypotheses to validate—not claims that either channel will automatically succeed.

### Recommended first-quarter focus

- **B2C beachhead A — Pet and relationship-led products:** personalised pet keepsakes, adoption/gotcha-day and memorial designs, pet-parent wall-art sets, and giftable cards. Use real customer details only with consent and a clear deletion/retention policy.
- **B2C beachhead B — Seasonal and celebration printables:** distinctive greeting cards, printable gift tags, decorative ornament designs, party games, and group activity packs. For Etsy, do not use AI-generated functional instructions, patterns, recipes, or project plans; human-authored instructions must be tested and shown to work.
- **B2C beachhead C — Differentiated learning and activity packs:** parent/educator-focused resources, reading challenges, subject-specific activity packs, and illustrated informational books. Require factual and age-appropriateness review by a qualified person. Platform rules differ; check each platform before publication.
- **B2B beachhead — Niche Publishing Launch Sprint:** a fixed-scope service that turns a buyer’s expertise or niche idea into a researched product brief, a small prototype, a production/QA pack, and a marketplace launch plan. Test willingness to pay before building a self-serve SaaS product.

### Objectives for the first 90 days

1. Interview 15–25 prospective buyers across consumer sellers and B2B publisher segments.
2. Research 30–50 micro-niche candidates, using platform search data, competing offers, reviews, trend evidence, and buyer interviews.
3. Launch approximately 12–20 carefully QA-gated consumer SKUs across three tightly related collections—not hundreds of auto-generated listings.
4. Sell 3–5 paid B2B pilot engagements, or document why the offer does not convert and revise it.
5. Establish a dashboard joining keyword evidence, production costs, quality issues, listing performance, advertising, customer feedback, and net contribution.
6. Decide which one or two product groups deserve a second-quarter investment based on sales, conversion, customer feedback, and unit economics.

These are operating targets for an experiment, not sales forecasts.

### 2.2 Evidence hierarchy for deciding what to make

Use these signals in descending order of strength:

1. **Paid transactions and repeat purchases** on a live test or paid pilot.
2. **Marketplace search demand plus credible competing sales signals**, such as recent reviews, bestseller indicators where available, and active competitor listings.
3. **Buyer interviews and observed behaviour**, including what buyers currently pay to solve the problem.
4. **Search trend, saves, social engagement, or viral discussion.** Useful for discovery, but weaker than purchase evidence.
5. **Founder intuition or AI-generated opportunity scoring.** Use only to create testable hypotheses.

Do not claim a product has “high demand and low competition” until you have collected the measurements. For each proposed niche, record the date, region, keyword, search count, number of competing listings, representative price range, competitor review/sales signals, buyer problem, and confidence level. A promising niche is one where there is enough demand to support the business, but competitor products leave a specific gap in fit, quality, format, access, age range, style, or personalisation.

### 2.3 Market sizing: use a bottom-up model

Public platform reports do not cleanly disclose the market size for AI-assisted printable products, and total digital-download TAM estimates can be misleading. Avoid a headline TAM that combines unrelated markets such as books, art, templates and classroom resources.

For each micro-niche, estimate a **serviceable obtainable market (SOM)** using observed data:

`Monthly orders = relevant keyword searches × observed listing click share × product-page conversion rate`

`Monthly gross revenue = monthly orders × realised average order value`

`Monthly contribution = gross revenue − platform/payment fees − refunds − ad cost − variable production/fulfilment cost`

Initially, click share and conversion will be uncertain. Use ranges, record assumptions and replace the estimates with actual listing analytics. Use Etsy’s own stats where possible; for other platforms, use the platform's actual reporting or controlled tests.

## 3. Strategic positioning and business model

### 3.1 Positioning statement

**For specific audiences with an unmet creative, educational, gifting or publishing need, we develop well-researched, carefully curated publishing products and launch-ready collections—using AI to accelerate production while retaining human judgement, quality assurance and market accountability.**

This is stronger than positioning the business as an “AI book factory.” Buyers do not normally want AI output; they want a gift that feels personal, an activity that works, a book that is enjoyable and accurate, or a faster route to a commercial product.

### 3.2 Two engines, one core system

| Business engine | Customer | Offer | Primary value | Revenue model | Main risk |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Consumer catalogue | Parents, gift buyers, pet owners, hobbyists, educators and readers | Distinctive printables, books, art, activities and themed collections | Specific fit, convenience, personal meaning, quality, originality | Single item, bundle, series, seasonal release, personalised order | Discovery and conversion are uncertain; low-priced goods can be fee- and ad-sensitive |
| B2B publishing service | Self-publishers, niche sellers, educators, coaches, creators, small publishers and agencies | Research, product concept, content/asset creation, QA and launch package | Lower production friction, faster testing, coherent product quality, reduced coordination overhead | Fixed-scope setup package, recurring managed service, later licence/subscription | Client acquisition, trust, scope creep, variable review effort |
| Later: framework-enabled product/service | Established sellers with repeated publishing demand | Repeatable niche research, production and reporting workflow | Predictable production cadence and product portfolio decisions | Monthly retainer, team licence, or SaaS only if demand is validated | Building software before proving willingness to pay; integration/support complexity |

### 3.3 Defensible advantage

AI image and text generation is becoming widely available. Defensibility must come from the combination of:

- **A niche intelligence loop:** systematic search, review, trend and competitor-gap research linked to sales outcomes.
- **Editorial and production standards:** factual audit, print QA, puzzle correctness, style consistency, accessibility and transparent product descriptions.
- **Collections, not isolated files:** series, companion products, bundles and cross-sell paths for a particular audience.
- **Audience knowledge:** buyer interviews, reviews, requests, repeat-purchase data and first-party email relationships.
- **Reliable delivery:** clear file specifications, accurate previews, prompt support, meaningful licensing terms, and quick fixes.
- **Operational learning:** actual production hours, defect types, conversion and customer feedback feed back into the framework.

### 3.4 Recommended business architecture

Do not operate one undifferentiated shop for children’s books, adult mandalas, pet portraits, stock photography, B2B services and educational resources. Each audience expects different trust signals and product language. Use separate storefronts or clearly distinct brand collections when the audience and platform positioning differ. A single backend can manage research and production, while the public-facing brands remain tightly focused.

### 4.2 Why not lead with every book and art category?

Your framework can support many formats, but each additional category introduces a different customer, quality standard, platform policy, keyword set, and marketing channel. A storybook needs character continuity and an engaging narrative; an educational book needs subject review; a puzzle book needs correctness; wall art needs style cohesion and accurate previews; a printable party game needs tested rules; stock art needs asset-level licensing and metadata discipline. Shared automation does not remove these distinct quality gates.

Use the broad framework behind the scenes, but focus the commercial offer on a small number of audience problems until the business has reliable conversion data.

## 5. Customer segments and their buying triggers

### 5.1 Consumer segments

**A. Emotion-led gift buyers**  
They buy for a relationship, memory, identity or occasion: pet parents, people celebrating a new pet or remembering one, friend groups, family gatherings, teachers, and hobby communities. Their purchase trigger is emotional relevance and a sense that the item was made for the recipient. Listing images should show the finished result and explain personalisation clearly.

**B. Convenience-led parents and hosts**  
They buy because they need a ready-to-use activity or resource under time pressure. Their purchase trigger is clear usefulness: age range, duration, number of pages/activities, what is included, materials needed, printing format and the problem solved. Clarity and tested functionality matter more than sheer page count.

**C. Hobbyists and self-directed learners**  
They buy for an absorbing experience, skill progression, challenge, style, or identity. Their purchase trigger is a specific topic and a credible quality promise (for example, verified solutions, a consistent illustration style, or a difficulty progression).

**D. Decor buyers**  
They buy for a coherent aesthetic, a room/purpose and a size that fits their printer or frame. Bundle consistency, mockups that accurately represent the files, ratio options and high-resolution delivery are key.

### 5.2 B2B segments

| Segment | Pain likely to be tested | Offer to test | How to reach them |
|---|---|---|---|
| Existing Etsy or digital-product sellers | Too slow to research and release new products; weak differentiation; inconsistent listing quality | A 2-week niche/product sprint with evidence, prototype assets, metadata and QA checklist | Etsy seller communities where promotion is allowed, creator communities, relevant newsletters, public case studies, opt-in outreach |
| Self-publishers and small presses | Editing, illustration, interior layout, series consistency, metadata and launch coordination | One-title pilot or three-title micro-series, with explicit human approval gates | KDP/indie-publishing groups, LinkedIn, author associations, podcast/newsletter partnerships |

| Agencies and established brands | They need high-variation creative assets and repeatable campaign collections | White-label production with brand guardrails and a documented QA/rights process | Agency partnerships, design/marketing consultants, referrals |
| Aspiring sellers with little production experience | Confusion, confidence bottleneck, fear of wasting money on the wrong niche | Product validation workshop plus a small launch-ready pilot | Demonstration content, case studies, a free niche validation checklist and paid consultation |

Do not market the offer as “earn passive income with AI” or imply guaranteed sales. Sell a reliable process, tested deliverables and a better-informed launch decision.

## 6. Market research and validation operating system

This should become a repeatable pipeline inside the existing framework.

### 6.1 Research stages

**Stage 1 — Audience/problem definition**

- State the buyer, moment, desired outcome and current alternative in one sentence.
- Identify whether the need is emotional, practical, educational, decorative, entertaining or commercial.
- Gather exact buyer language from public reviews, forums and interviews; remove personal data and do not republish private content.
- Write the product promise without mentioning AI unless AI is materially relevant to the buyer.

**Stage 2 — Demand collection**

- Etsy: use Marketplace Insights for primary keywords and related search terms; record searches and listings for the last 30 days. The current free allowance is 15 searches weekly; use it on the highest-value candidate terms first.
- Pinterest: use region-specific search trends and historical curves, particularly for decor, gifts, aesthetics and crafts.
- Amazon: inspect current search results, product positioning, reviews, pricing, categories and available sales/bestseller indicators for the relevant book format.
- TikTok Creative Center: use trendlines, related hashtags, audience information and top-ad examples as creative/discovery signals, not sales proof.
- Google Trends and keyword tools: compare seasonality, regional interest and related phrases; treat normalized trend indices as comparative rather than absolute market size.
- Direct research: interview buyers and read negative reviews to find missing features, quality frustrations and repeated requests.

**Stage 3 — Competition and gap analysis**

For the top 5–10 competing offers per niche, record: product promise, audience specificity, price, bundle size, preview quality, format, recent-review signals where visible, buyer complaints, personalization, delivery, accessibility and gaps. Do not scrape or reproduce competitors’ content. The purpose is to identify a gap, not to clone a successful listing.

**Stage 4 — Product prototype**

Generate a small sample before creating a full book or large bundle. For a 50-page book, validate the cover concept, 5 representative interior pages, table of contents, metadata and print layout first. For a printable pack, show a true preview and one complete sample activity. For B2B, produce a paid discovery deliverable instead of building a large free demonstration.

**Stage 5 — Pre-launch test**

Use an opt-in waitlist, buyer interviews, small paid pilots, marketplace listings with a limited initial inventory, or a low-budget ad test. Avoid inferring purchase intent from likes alone. Do not create fake demand, fake reviews, false scarcity, or misleading mockups.

**Stage 6 — Release gate**

A product can launch only when it has: (a) recorded demand evidence, (b) a defined target buyer, (c) a differentiated promise, (d) rights/policy approval, (e) content and technical QA, (f) accurate preview assets, (g) a price and margin calculation, and (h) a measurable marketing plan.

## 8. Channel-specific marketing and acquisition strategy

### 8.1 Etsy: intent-led listings, not upload volume

**Discovery strategy**

- Research buyer terms with Marketplace Insights before creating a listing; use specific buyer/problem/occasion wording rather than generic strings such as “beautiful printable.”
- Design each listing around a single promise, audience and use case. Put format, file types, sizes, page count, personalization requirements, print limitations and delivery method in the description.
- Show the product faithfully: actual pages, close-up details, print-size comparisons, what is included and a photo of a real printout where appropriate. Do not fabricate physical inventory with a mockup that implies an item exists when it does not.
- Use coherent variations and bundles to increase value, but ensure every variation is genuinely different and useful.
- Disclose AI use when required, describe the human creative role honestly, and check Etsy’s updated rules before each new category is listed.

**Important current policy gate:** Etsy’s 22 October 2026 update says seller-prompted AI creations can continue to be sold when there is individual human creative input and AI is disclosed. Bot-generated listings without individual human involvement are prohibited. AI-generated functional instructions—including patterns, recipes, construction/project plans, assembly manuals and instructional guides—are not allowed. Functional instructions must be the seller’s own work; the seller should show a real result achieved by following the instructions. Etsy also introduced an unedited photo requirement for listings, with limited product-dependent exceptions. Read the full [AI creation guidance](https://www.etsy.com/sg-en/seller-handbook/article/1275449912004) and [official announcement](https://www.etsy.com/news/creativity-and-trust-in-the-age-of-ai) before launch. These requirements are new and may be clarified further.

**Advertising**

- Do not turn on large campaigns for unproven listings. First establish that the thumbnail and offer earn clicks and that the product page converts.
- Test a few relevant listings and keywords at a small, pre-authorised loss limit. Record ads, attribution window, fee impact and profit after advertising.
- Etsy Offsite Ads charge 15% on attributed orders for shops below US$10,000 in Etsy revenue in any consecutive 365-day period (eligible to opt out) and 12% at or above that level (mandatory for the lifetime of the shop). Check the shop’s current status and include the fee in margin models. [Official explanation](https://help.etsy.com/hc/en-us/articles/360000338367-How-Etsy-s-Offsite-Ads-Work).

### 8.2 Pinterest: visual search and evergreen traffic

Best products: coordinated wall-art sets, gift collections, pet art, holiday craft inspiration, family activity pages, colouring samples and other visual products.

Publish vertical pins showing (a) the finished result, (b) what is inside the download, (c) a real use context, and (d) the specific problem or occasion. Create separate pins for different search intents rather than reusing the same generic creative. Use keyword-rich board and pin descriptions naturally. Link each pin directly to the matching product or a focused landing page.

Measure impressions, saves, pin clicks, outbound clicks, outbound-click rate and purchases. Saves are useful for creative resonance; **outbound clicks and sales are the commercial measures**. Compare a 30-day baseline and use Pinterest Trends’ regional historical data for timing. Avoid judging a product based only on viral reach.

### 8.3 TikTok, Instagram Reels and YouTube Shorts: demonstrate the transformation

These channels suit products whose value can be understood visually in seconds. Use short, faceless formats when useful:

- Before/after: a buyer need, rough concept, finished page or coordinated collection.
- “What’s inside”: flip through a book or show the files in use.
- Time-lapse: design-to-print workflow with real, representative outputs.
- “One niche, three products”: demonstrate the value of hyper-specific collections.
- For B2B: show how a blank idea becomes a validated product brief, prototype, QA report and launch assets—without exposing private client information.

Use TikTok Creative Center to review trendlines, related hashtags, audiences and examples of high-performing creatives. Do not hijack a short-lived viral topic if it involves protected characters, celebrity likenesses, brand assets, copyrighted text, or a trademarked phrase. Use trends for broad themes and buyer language, not for IP infringement. [TikTok Creative Center](https://ads.tiktok.com/resources/help/article/about-creative-center?redirected=2).

### 8.4 Amazon KDP: optimise the book page, then test ads

- Choose one clear audience, reader outcome, format and differentiation point.
- Design the cover and sample pages to show exactly what is inside. Avoid repeating near-identical material across multiple books; KDP’s content-quality guidance identifies excessively reused or repeated content and poor customer experience as problems.
- For puzzles, validate the puzzles and answer keys programmatically and independently; then sample-check manually.
- For children's and non-fiction books, review facts, reading level, typography, image/text consistency and layout. Use Kindle Previewer and printed proofs where relevant.
- Disclose AI-generated text, images or translations as required by KDP. Keep an internal record of models/tools, licences, human edits, source notes and approval decisions.
- After the product detail page is sound, test Sponsored Products and report spend, attributed sales, royalty and contribution by keyword/ad group. Stop keywords that spend beyond the pre-set limit without sufficient evidence of conversion.

### 8.5 Direct channels and retention

The direct store provides control and customer relationships but requires demand generation. Begin with Payhip or Gumroad for a low-friction test, or use Shopify once catalogue size and sales justify its recurring fee. Use a small free sample or useful checklist to create an **opt-in** email list; follow platform rules and privacy/anti-spam requirements.

Lifecycle sequence:

1. Sample download or useful checklist → clear consent and expected email frequency.
2. Welcome email → the problem solved, one best product and a helpful sample.
3. Collection email → related products and honest use cases.
4. Seasonal/launch email → relevant offer, deadline only when genuine.
5. Post-purchase → download help, request feedback, offer a useful companion product.
6. Repeat customer → collection launch, bundle or licence upgrade.

Never use fake reviews, invented testimonials, or false claims of scarcity. Ask for honest feedback without conditioning a reward on a positive review. Keep support and refund paths easy to find.

### 8.6 B2B buyer acquisition: sell proof, not AI hype

**Offer ladder to test**

- **Free lead magnet:** a niche validation checklist or mini opportunity report explaining how search, competition and review gaps are assessed.
- **Paid discovery / audit:** a fixed price for an opportunity shortlist plus an evidence dossier and a recommendation to proceed, revise or stop.
- **Niche Publishing Launch Sprint:** a one-off, tightly scoped package with a validated brief, one prototype, structured QA and launch assets.
- **Collection build:** a larger engagement for a coherent multi-product series with client review points.
- **Managed publishing partner:** recurring work once a client has demonstrated repeated need and the workflow is profitable.

**Illustrative price tests—not established market rates:** test a discovery offer around A$150–A$300; a compact launch sprint around A$400–A$900; and a larger collection engagement around A$1,500–A$4,000. These are pricing hypotheses to interview and sell against, not promises that customers will accept them. Let early buyer conversations and paid pilots establish the actual scope, price, turnaround and margin.

**Outreach approach**

- Build a list of 50 qualified prospects with a visible publishing or product-creation problem.
- Personalise outreach around one observable gap, not a generic claim that AI will transform their business.
- Offer a useful insight or mini audit, then invite a short discovery call.
- Use a one-page example that shows evidence → gap → prototype → QA → launch assets. Use only your own work or authorised case studies.
- Ask every pilot customer what they previously paid for, how long it took, what failed, which deliverable mattered most, and whether they would pay again.
- Track prospect-to-reply, reply-to-call, call-to-paid-pilot and repeat-engagement rates. Stop channels that deliver unqualified leads.

## 9. Advertising and experimentation playbook

### 9.1 Launch sequence

1. **Organic product-page test:** publish a small number of complete, accurate offers and improve the main image, title, preview, promise and price based on behaviour.
2. **Creative test:** for the same product, test a small number of materially different thumbnail/intro concepts where platform rules permit. Do not change several variables at once if the result needs to be interpretable.
3. **Audience/keyword test:** promote a proven listing or landing page with a limited budget and a clear stopping point.
4. **Retargeting:** only where the audience size, privacy/consent requirements and platform rules make it appropriate.
5. **Scale:** allocate budget to positive-contribution products, not simply high-reach products.

### 9.2 Unit economics and ad thresholds

Use contribution after **all variable costs**, not gross sales, to decide what to advertise.

`Contribution per order = net price received − platform/payment fees − variable generation/fulfilment cost − expected refund/support cost`

`First-order contribution after ads = contribution before ads − customer acquisition cost (CAC)`

`Break-even ROAS = 1 ÷ contribution-margin rate before ads`

For example, if a product has a 70% contribution margin before advertising, the break-even ROAS is approximately 1.43× (1 / 0.70), before fixed overhead and tax. This is a calculation, not an industry benchmark. The business should normally target a buffer above break-even to cover fixed costs and uncertainty.

For low-priced printables, fixed payment fees and acquisition costs can consume the margin. Test bundles and collections to improve average order value, but only if buyers perceive the additional value.

### 9.3 Weekly review agenda

- Which search phrases and pins generated qualified outbound clicks?
- Which product pages earned sales, not just clicks?
- What was net contribution after marketplace and advertising fees?
- Which customer questions, refunds or complaints repeated?
- Did the listing preview correctly represent the file?
- What quality defects escaped into a shipped product?
- Which niche assumptions are now supported or contradicted by evidence?
- What should be improved, bundled, paused or discontinued next week?

## 10. Recommended enhancements to the AI publishing framework

The framework already performs research, ideation, content creation, audit, audience/SEO strategy and image generation. The highest-value enhancements are likely to be controls and measurement rather than another content-generation model.

### 10.1 Marketplace policy and rights gate

Create a versioned policy registry by platform and product format. The registry should capture the source URL, last-checked date, policy version/summary, AI disclosure requirements, automation restrictions, functional-instruction rules, image-preview requirements, rights rules and approval owner. If a policy was last checked too long ago, block publication and require a fresh review.

Use an IP risk classifier as a **warning system**, not a legal clearance guarantee. Flag named characters, franchises, trademarks, branded product names, celebrity likenesses, living artists’ names, copyrighted song lyrics, proprietary patterns and requests to imitate a specific protected work. Allow a human to resolve ambiguous results. Keep a documented provenance/rights record for each product.

### 10.2 Human-in-the-loop workflow

The agent can research, draft, generate options, prepare metadata and package files. A person must approve:

- The actual product concept and its audience promise.
- The final creative output and collection coherence.
- Facts, answer keys, instructional material and age suitability.
- Rights/policy checks and any platform disclosures.
- Listing text, preview images, price and publication decision.

This is particularly important for Etsy, whose new policy prohibits bot-generated listings without individual human prompting, curation and creative input for each item. Avoid fully automatic catalogue publishing. Use the agent to reduce effort but require a per-SKU review record.

### 10.3 QA pipelines by format

| Format | Automated checks | Human checks |
|---|---|---|
| Children's/teen books | Spell/grammar checks, reading-level indicators, source/reference consistency, image/text alignment flags, trim/margin checks | Factual review, age suitability, narrative quality, inclusivity, illustration continuity, actual reading experience |
| Puzzle/activity books | Solver/verifier for every puzzle, duplicate detection, answer-key check, pagination/margin checks | Sample solve, difficulty calibration, instruction clarity and usability |
| Colouring books/line art | Image resolution, line contrast, blank-page/duplicate detection, crop and bleed checks | Line clarity, visual interest, print suitability, no accidental grey fills or awkward cut-offs |
| Wall art/printables | Aspect-ratio variants, file integrity, resolution, colour profile, filename/ZIP checks | Actual print test, scale and legibility, colour accuracy expectations, cohesive collection and accurate preview |
| Gift cards/tags | Text spelling, safe margins, dimensions, PDF preview and file integrity | Tone appropriateness, personalisation accuracy, print test, accurate description |
| B2B client work | Checklist completion, source/link manifest, version comparison, file export checks | Client approval, rights and confidentiality, brand match, scope completion and acceptance |

### 10.4 Production observability and feedback

For every job and product, log: model/tool versions, input prompt and constraints, source evidence, generation attempts, accepted/rejected variants, human time, compute/tool cost, audit results, policy review, file hash/version, listing URL, publication date, sales, refunds, support incidents, ad spend and customer feedback. This lets the framework learn which processes are efficient and which output types produce repeat problems.

Do not optimise simply for images or SKUs produced per hour. Optimise for **approved products with demonstrated demand and positive contribution per total production hour**.

### 10.5 Licensing, localisation and accessibility modules

- Add personal-use, classroom-use, small-business commercial-use and custom licence templates, each reviewed for the intended platform and jurisdiction.
- Make regional spelling, paper sizes (US Letter/A4), units, date formats, language variants and age/grade terminology selectable.
- For educational content, include reading-level/age range, accessible typography, alt-text or accessible PDF handling where relevant, and human-reviewed language versions.
- Maintain source notes and a fact-check record for non-fiction; never let an agent silently invent citations.
- Maintain clear retention rules for uploaded customer photographs and private business material. Delete source data when no longer required, except for records the business legitimately needs to retain.

## 11. Operations and production workflow

### 11.1 Standard production stages

1. **Research intake:** a documented opportunity brief and evidence score.
2. **Go/no-go:** human decides whether the problem, audience and differentiation warrant a prototype.
3. **Prototype:** cover/listing concept, sample pages and product outline.
4. **Content and asset production:** versioned generation with prompt constraints, source references and selected variants.
5. **Audit:** automated plus human QA suitable for the format.
6. **Packaging:** export correct formats, file sizes, print dimensions, licence, usage notes and accessibility variants.
7. **Listing preparation:** platform-specific metadata, AI disclosures, authentic images/previews, price and policy checklist.
8. **Human publication approval:** explicit per-item sign-off; no unreviewed bot-generated batch publication.
9. **Launch and measure:** record baseline and channel-specific outcomes.
10. **Improve/retire:** update, bundle, relaunch, or retire based on evidence and customer experience.

### 11.2 Roles

In the first phase, one person may handle several roles, but keep the responsibilities distinct in the workflow:

- Market analyst / niche researcher.
- Editorial/subject-matter reviewer.
- Visual art director / collection curator.
- Production and formatting QA.
- Platform/policy/IP reviewer.
- Growth and ads manager.
- Customer support and feedback analyst.
- B2B sales and account lead.

Automation can prepare decisions and reduce repeated manual work. It should not erase accountability for final publication, customer claims or third-party rights.

### 11.3 Customer support standard

Prepare templates for download trouble, printing, sizing, personalisation correction, wrong-file delivery, refund requests, licence questions and B2B revision boundaries. Track the support time per order. If buyers repeatedly misunderstand the product, fix the listing and preview rather than scaling the advertising.

## 12. Pricing and financial feasibility

All figures in this section are planning examples in Australian dollars unless stated otherwise. They are **illustrative assumptions, not forecasts or verified market prices**. Validate with actual platform fee calculators, live tests, tax advice and buyer interviews.

### 12.1 Consumer price architecture to test

- Single printable/art file: approximately A$4–A$9.
- A more complete occasion or activity bundle: approximately A$9–A$19.
- Higher-value personalised digital keepsake: approximately A$12–A$35, depending on scope and revisions.
- Paperback book: price only after calculating print cost, royalty and competitive positioning; avoid assuming that a large page count automatically justifies a higher price.
- Direct business licence or expanded classroom/small-business rights: price separately only when demand exists and terms are clear.

These are starting test bands for product planning, not conclusions derived from marketplace averages.

### 12.2 Etsy fee example

Etsy currently lists a US$0.20 listing fee, a 6.5% transaction fee and location-dependent payment processing. For an Australian bank account, the published processing rate is 3% + A$0.25 for domestic orders and 4% + A$0.25 for international orders. An Offsite Ads fee of 15% or 12% may apply to attributed orders depending on shop revenue. Confirm current rates and whether taxes apply to fees. Sources: [Etsy Australia fees](https://www.etsy.com/au/legal/fees), [Etsy Payments rates](https://www.etsy.com/legal/etsy-payments/), [Offsite Ads](https://help.etsy.com/hc/en-us/articles/360000338367-How-Etsy-s-Offsite-Ads-Work).

Simple illustrative domestic order, before tax, currency effects, refunds, labour or ads:

| Item price | 6.5% transaction fee | 3% + A$0.25 processing | Approx. listing fee equivalent | Approx. amount after these fees* |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| A$7.00 | A$0.46 | A$0.46 | A$0.29 | A$5.79 |
| A$15.00 | A$0.98 | A$0.70 | A$0.29 | A$13.03 |

*The listing fee equivalent varies with exchange rates because Etsy charges the listing fee in USD. These examples exclude Offsite Ads, applicable taxes on fees, any currency conversion charge, refunds, support and production cost. If the sale attracts a 15% Offsite Ads fee, subtract an additional A$1.05 on the A$7 sale or A$2.25 on the A$15 sale. This is why high-value bundles and direct repeat customers may be more viable than very cheap standalone files, provided the bundle has genuine buyer value.

### 12.3 Platform cost choices

- **Etsy:** discovery advantage, but listing, transaction, processing and potential advertising fees.
- **Payhip:** free plan has 5% platform transaction fee plus payment processor charges; use while testing direct conversion.
- **Gumroad:** direct sales 10% + US$0.50 plus processing; Discovery 30%. Better when its convenience outweighs per-order fees.
- **Shopify:** recurring monthly cost plus processing; use when direct sales justify the expense and you can generate traffic.
- **KDP:** no upfront printed inventory under print-on-demand, but actual royalties depend on price, printing costs, sales territory and format.

### 12.4 Financial scenarios, not promises

Treat these as a spreadsheet template for planning. Replace the assumptions with actual data after the first 30–90 days.

| Scenario | Consumer units/month | Average consumer order | Consumer gross/month | B2B paid projects/month | B2B average fee | B2B gross/month | Total gross/month |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Pilot | 40 | A$9 | A$360 | 1 | A$400 | A$400 | A$760 |
| Evidence of fit | 250 | A$10 | A$2,500 | 4 | A$750 | A$3,000 | A$5,500 |

Consumer platform fees, ads, refunds and support must be deducted from consumer gross. ### 12.5 What the framework must measure to prove profitability

For consumer products, calculate:

- Realised price, units and refund rate.
- Marketplace/payment fees and currency conversion.
- Paid-ad cost per sale and attributed gross sales.
- Generation/tool cost per approved product.
- Human research, QA, listing and support time.
- Contribution by SKU, collection, channel and cohort.

For B2B, calculate:

- Qualified lead cost and sales hours.
- Proposal-to-paid conversion.
- Delivery hours versus the fee.
- Revision and support load.
- Contribution per project and repeat rate.
- Actual customer outcome, without attributing unverified revenue gains to the framework.

### 12.6 Break-even rule

Do not continue a product simply because it was cheap to generate. Continue or expand when it has credible demand evidence, customer satisfaction, manageable support requirements and positive contribution after variable costs. A product with weak sales may still be useful as a lead magnet or bundle component, but that role should be explicit and measured.

## 13. Legal, platform, copyright and trust requirements

This is an operating checklist, not legal or tax advice. Confirm requirements for each sales region and product type before launch.

### 13.1 Etsy AI rules: current as of 26 October 2026

Etsy’s policy update of 22 October 2026 is especially important for this framework:

- AI art can still be permitted when the seller has a clear creative role and discloses AI use.
- Bot-generated listings without individual human involvement are prohibited; Etsy says each item must involve individual human prompting, curation and creative input.
- AI-generated functional instructions are prohibited, including craft patterns, recipes, project/build plans, assembly manuals and instructional guides. The information buyers rely on to achieve the result must be the seller’s own work.
- The updated listing-image rules require an unedited photo of the actual item against a real background, with product-dependent exceptions, and all images must accurately represent what the buyer receives.

Sources: [Etsy AI creation guidance](https://www.etsy.com/sg-en/seller-handbook/article/1275449912004), [Etsy announcement](https://www.etsy.com/news/creativity-and-trust-in-the-age-of-ai), [Etsy Creativity Standards](https://www.etsy.com/legal/creativity). Re-check these immediately before listing; the update is recent and may receive clarifications.

**Practical consequence:** keep automation inside research, generation, QA assistance, metadata drafting and file preparation. Use an explicit human approval step for each Etsy product and listing. For DIY products, distinguish decorative artwork from instructions/patterns; human-authored instructions should be tested and documented. Do not use automated agents to mass-post hundreds of thin variants.

### 13.2 Amazon KDP

KDP requires disclosure of AI-generated text, images and translations at publication or republishing. AI-assisted editing, error-checking and ideation of human-created material are treated differently under its guidance. KDP also makes publishers responsible for content quality and rights compliance. Maintain an internal record of the generation process and select the correct KDP disclosure response. Sources: [KDP content guidelines](https://kdp.amazon.com/en_US/help/topic/G200672390), [Kindle content quality guide](https://kdp.amazon.com/en_US/help/topic/G200952510).

### 13.3 Copyright and AI-generated outputs

Copyright rules vary by jurisdiction. The US Copyright Office’s January 2025 report states that purely AI-generated material is not protected by copyright in the same way as human-authored expression, while sufficient human-authored expression, creative selection/arrangement or modification can support protection. Australian copyright guidance also treats sufficient human creative input as relevant, but a given output’s status can be fact-specific. Do not claim exclusive copyright in a purely generated image without an appropriate legal basis; retain records of meaningful human decisions and modifications. Sources: [US Copyright Office report announcement](https://www.copyright.gov/newsnet/2025/1060.html), [Copyright Agency Australia AI Q&A](https://www.copyright.com.au/membership/ai-and-copyright-new-licensing-opportunities-for-members/ai-qa/).

### 13.4 IP and likeness screening

- Do not build a business model around copyrighted characters, franchises, logos, celebrity portraits or protected branded phrases unless the appropriate rights are secured.
- Avoid prompts, titles and keywords that name living artists or copyrighted works as a shortcut to audience demand. Adobe Stock explicitly restricts certain names and copyrighted-work references in AI asset prompts/metadata.
- Check trademarks for brand names and product series, and rights to fonts, stock assets, reference images, music, models and third-party components.
- Keep provenance, licence terms, prompts, edits, generation date and human approval record.
- A tool’s commercial-use licence does not automatically resolve a third-party rights claim.

### 13.5 EU AI Act transparency

The European Commission says AI Act Article 50 transparency obligations began applying on 2 August 2026, with certain machine-readable marking obligations for systems already on the market subject to a limited grace period until 2 December 2026. Scope and obligations depend on the provider/deployer and type of content/system. If selling to EU customers or operating an AI system within scope, assess applicability with appropriate legal advice rather than assuming a marketplace disclosure alone is sufficient. [European Commission FAQ](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/faqs/transparency-obligations-under-article-50-ai-act).

### 13.6 Australian tax, consumer law and privacy

- For Australian business operations, monitor GST turnover: the ATO states that GST registration is generally required when current or projected GST turnover reaches A$75,000, subject to detailed rules and exceptions. A business already registered for GST should obtain accountant advice before changing registration status. [ATO GST registration](https://www.ato.gov.au/businesses-and-organisations/gst-excise-and-indirect-taxes/gst/registering-for-gst).
- Australian consumer law applies to online purchases; product descriptions and advertising must not be false or misleading, and consumer guarantees may apply. [ACCC online buying guidance](https://www.accc.gov.au/consumers/buying-products-and-services/buying-online).
- Use clear terms for digital downloads, refunds, licences and support. Obtain appropriate permissions to use customer photographs and personal details. Limit retention and access to personal data; protect source files and client materials.
- Keep bookkeeping records for marketplace payouts, fees, refunds, advertising, software and contractors. Marketplace tax collection rules differ by jurisdiction; do not assume a platform remits every tax obligation for the seller.

## 14. Gap analysis: what is missing from a production-focused framework

| Gap | Why it matters | Recommended enhancement | Priority |
|---|---|---|---|
| Customer validation before full production | Cheap generation can still create unsold inventory and wasted attention | Prototype gate, buyer interviews, paid pilots, keyword evidence and explicit no-go decisions | Critical |
| Platform rule versioning | Rules change; Etsy’s recent AI update demonstrates direct commercial risk | Versioned policy registry, periodic recheck, human sign-off and blocked publication when stale | Critical |
| Human contribution and disclosure record | Etsy requires individual creative input and AI disclosure; KDP requires disclosure of AI-generated content | Per-SKU creation record, human changes, approvals, disclosure fields and accurate listing photos | Critical |
| IP provenance and licence tracking | Commercial-use rights and third-party IP are not interchangeable | Rights manifest for fonts, stock assets, models, source text, generated assets and client materials | Critical |
| Format-specific QA | A visually polished book can still contain wrong answers, factual errors or broken printing | Distinct QA validators and human acceptance criteria for every format | Critical |
| Demand and profit feedback loop | “Number of products created” does not measure business value | SKU economics, conversion, sales attribution, refund/support, quality-defect and production-hour dashboard | Critical |
| Listing representation and trust | Buyers cannot assess a digital file if only a generic AI mockup is shown | Actual previews, sample pages, honest mockups, dimensions, file types and real printed samples where applicable | High |
| B2B sales process | A great framework does not automatically create qualified leads or trust | Narrow offer, buyer interviews, case study, scope/pricing templates, CRM and referral mechanism | High |
| Brand and audience architecture | Diverse categories can confuse shoppers and weaken relevance | Brand by buyer/job; collections and series within each brand | High |
| Retention and customer ownership | Marketplace discovery can be volatile and repeat sales are hard to capture | Consent-based email, direct store, bundles, new-release cadence and clear licences | High |
| Pricing and licensing tiers | A low-priced file may not cover fees, ads and support; commercial use needs clarity | Contribution calculator and legally reviewed licence options | High |
| Accessibility and localisation | Files may be hard to read, unusable on local paper sizes, or culturally mismatched | US Letter/A4 variants, accessible typography, age/grade settings, language review and user testing | Medium-high |
| Analytics attribution | Views from one platform may not convert; ads may be misattributed | UTM conventions, channel dashboard, cohort analysis and profit-based reporting | High |
| Customer privacy | Custom pet photos, client manuscripts and business files may be sensitive | Consent, retention/deletion policy, least-privilege storage and clear privacy notice | High |
| Content decay and catalogue maintenance | Seasonal products expire; policies, facts, metadata or links change | Review schedule, update/retire flag, broken-link checks, trend lifecycle and rights re-review | Medium-high |
| Sales tax and platform accounting | Gross marketplace deposits are not the same as revenue or profit | Finance ledger for sales, fees, refunds, currency and GST/tax advice | High |
| Trend-jacking/IP boundary | Fast trends create temptation to use protected characters, celebrity names or brands | Trend-to-safe-concept transformation and mandatory rights review | Critical |
| Support burden and refund causes | Automation does not eliminate buyer confusion or defective files | Support templates, error taxonomy, product-page fixes and support-time measurement | Medium-high |
| Product lifecycle and cannibalisation | Large collections can compete with one another or flood the same query | Catalogue map, keyword ownership, bundle architecture and release calendar | Medium |
| Independent proof of B2B value | Claims about saving time or money may be persuasive but need evidence | Baseline/after metrics from pilots; permissioned case studies; no fabricated ROI promises | High |

## 15. 90-day execution roadmap

### Days 1–14: establish the evidence and risk controls

- Choose three consumer micro-niche clusters plus the B2B Launch Sprint offer.
- Set up the policy/rights registry and update it for Etsy’s 22 Oct 2026 changes and KDP’s AI disclosure requirements.
- Create the research scorecard, competitor analysis template, per-format QA checklist and unit-economics calculator.
- Interview at least 10 B2B prospects and 5 consumer/educator/parent/pet-owner buyers; seek specific previous spending, frustrations and buying triggers.
- Collect marketplace data for 30–50 candidates; select no more than 5 prototypes to build in depth.
- Create B2B offer page and sample dossier; make the scope, deliverables and exclusions explicit.

**Gate:** If a niche has no evidence beyond generic trend enthusiasm, do not invest in a full collection.

### Days 15–30: build and sell prototypes

- Produce 3–5 consumer prototypes per selected cluster, with QA proportional to format.
- Launch the first paid B2B discovery/Launch Sprint pilots; aim to learn willingness to pay and delivery hours, not maximise volume.
- Create honest listing images/previews, file specifications and AI disclosures where required.
- Open a direct checkout/waitlist for one bundle to measure interest and collect consent-based emails.
- Publish initial Pinterest pins and a small number of short-form demos showing actual product value.

**Gate:** Do not release a book or puzzle collection until representative pages and answers pass independent review.

### Days 31–60: test conversion and refine

- Publish the first carefully selected 12–20 SKUs if each has passed the release gate; avoid unreviewed mass uploads.
- Compare organic search, Pinterest outbound clicks, direct-store conversions and early sales.
- Test price/bundle positioning and improved listing thumbnails where permitted.
- Complete and debrief B2B pilots; document real time saved, production costs and client satisfaction only where measured.
- Remove or repair product pages where customer questions reveal unclear deliverables.
- Create a weekly dashboard and assign a decision to every SKU: improve, bundle, hold, expand or retire.

**Gate:** No scaling of ads unless the product page shows credible conversion and the contribution model remains positive after ad cost.

### Days 61–90: choose the winners and systematise

- Double down on the one or two collections with the best combined demand, conversion, contribution and review/support profile.
- Release companion products and bundles only where buyer behaviour suggests cross-sell value.
- Create one documented B2B case study, with client permission and defensible metrics.
- Decide whether to prioritise consumer catalogue scale, B2B service growth, or a balanced combination.
- Set next-quarter budget based on actual data; do not extrapolate a few sales into a market-wide forecast.

### Months 4–12: controlled growth

- Build series and companion products around validated audiences.
- Add KDP titles only after format-specific QA and print-cost checks.
- Add a second market/language only where localisation quality and real demand justify it.
- Introduce a recurring B2B package only if at least a few customers repeat or ask for ongoing delivery.
- Consider platform automation/API integration only where platform policies permit it and manual review remains enforceable.
- Revisit policy rules, pricing, trend fit and catalogue health quarterly.

## 16. Management dashboard and decision rules

| Metric | Why it matters | Review cadence | Decision use |
|---|---|---|---|
| Search interest and competing listings by keyword | Separates evidence from intuition | Before prototype; monthly for priority niches | Continue research, enter, or reject |
| Product-page impressions and click-through | Tests thumbnail/title fit | Weekly | Rework first image, title or audience promise |
| Product conversion rate | Tests offer and trust after a click | Weekly/monthly when enough traffic exists | Improve preview, price, description or product fit |
| Average order value and bundle take-rate | Tests value architecture | Monthly | Improve bundles and companion products |
| Contribution per order and per SKU | Reveals actual profitability | Monthly | Scale, hold, reprice or retire |
| CAC / break-even ROAS | Keeps paid growth within unit economics | Per campaign, weekly review | Increase, reduce or stop spend |
| Refunds and support contacts per 100 orders | Measures product clarity and defects | Weekly/monthly | Improve files, listing and QA rules |
| Quality defect escape rate | Measures failures missed before delivery | Each release; monthly trend | Strengthen validator or human QA |
| Production hours per approved SKU | Measures automation efficiency | Every job | Identify bottlenecks without sacrificing quality |
| B2B lead-to-paid conversion | Tests the service proposition | Monthly | Change segment, offer, proof or outreach |
| B2B contribution per delivery hour | Tests scale feasibility | Per project | Reprice, reduce scope, or productise steps |
| Repeat purchases / referrals | Indicates satisfaction and catalogue fit | Monthly/quarterly | Invest in series, retention and partnerships |

Suggested **internal guardrails**, not universal industry benchmarks:

- Any factual or answer-key defect in a published educational/puzzle product triggers a root-cause review.
- Do not scale paid ads while first-order contribution is negative unless a defined and measured repeat-purchase case justifies it.
- If a listing receives clicks but no sales after a meaningful and documented test, investigate fit, preview, price and trust before creating more variants.
- Pause a niche if the evidence remains weak after the agreed test budget/time window; do not defend it because it was easy to generate.
- Require explicit human approval for every platform publication and every B2B deliverable.

## 17. Principal risks and mitigations

| Risk | Likelihood / impact (initial assessment) | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| Overproduction before validating demand | High / high | Research gates, prototypes, limited releases and a stop/scale decision after each test |
| Marketplace policy changes | High / high | Policy registry, date/version checks, manual publication gate, channel diversification |
| Copyright/trademark infringement | Medium / very high | IP screening, licensed assets, provenance records, no protected-character trend-jacking and legal review for ambiguous cases |
| Low conversion despite high output volume | High / high | Audience-specific product promises, honest previews, buyer interviews, SEO evidence and conversion analytics |
| Generic/low-quality AI content damages trust | High / high | Human curation, collection-level art direction, factual review, duplicate detection, sample printing and customer feedback loop |
| Search trend spikes disappear before launch | Medium-high / medium-high | Small prototypes, trend half-life tracking, avoid overbuilding, maintain evergreen companion products |
| B2B offer fails to sell | Medium / high | Paid discovery pilots, narrow segment, validate willingness to pay before software investment |
| Paid ads cost more than margin | Medium-high / high | Unit-economics calculation, capped pilots, break-even ROAS and stopping rules |
| B2B client scope creep | Medium / medium-high | Fixed deliverables, revision caps, client approval milestones, written acceptance criteria |
| Personal data mishandling | Medium / high | Consent, secure storage, minimal retention and deletion policies |
| Revenue overestimated from platform-wide statistics | High / high | Bottom-up measurement; clearly label assumptions; forecast from actual sales only |

## 18. Final recommendation

The opportunity is credible enough to test, but **the winning business will be the one that learns fastest from real buyers—not the one that generates the most products**.

The best first move is a dual-track experiment:

- Launch a small, highly differentiated consumer catalogue in pet/relationship gifting and seasonal/occasion printables, using Etsy for marketplace intent and Pinterest/direct channels for discovery and retention.
- Launch the B2B Niche Publishing Launch Sprint as a paid service immediately, because it tests whether the framework can solve a valuable production and decision problem for sellers and publishers.
- Keep education, puzzle books, art collections and longer illustrated books in the portfolio, but add each only when the buyer, platform and QA requirements are well understood.
- Implement a policy/rights registry, mandatory per-product human approval, format-specific quality pipelines and actual contribution reporting before increasing output volume.

Within 90 days, the target is not to prove that every category works. It is to identify which audience and offer produces the strongest combination of real demand, quality, margin, repeat interest and manageable acquisition cost—and to build the next iteration around that evidence.
