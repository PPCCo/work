### [02-durability] Durable demand vs. passing fad

- **[02-durability]** Large-print senior puzzles (word search, sudoku, crossword) are structurally durable: an aging population, low screen tolerance among some seniors, and gift-giving occasions recur every year — but the _generic_ form of these books is now a commodity category with heavy price/quality competition. Confidence: high.
- **[02-durability]** Grayscale/photo-realistic coloring is presented by trend sources as a durable stylistic shift within adult coloring (better with modern markers/pencils, mixed-difficulty pages), not a one-season fad — but it is newer, so multi-year durability is `unverified`. Confidence: med.
- **[02-durability]** Generic mandala/geometric-pattern coloring, basic zentangle, and fairy-tale-character coloring books are explicitly flagged as declining niches in 2026 trend analysis, after roughly a decade as the default adult-coloring subject. Treat these as a fading category unless paired with a fresh sub-theme. Confidence: med (single detailed trend source, consistent with anecdotal saturation elsewhere).
- **[02-durability]** Murder-mystery/elimination logic puzzles are a hot current micro-trend visible across multiple new-release charts; this pattern (a single puzzle mechanic suddenly flooding new-release feeds) is a classic KDP fad signature — likely durable in modest form but at high risk of feed-saturation within 12–24 months. Confidence: med.
- **[02-durability]** Gratitude journals, generic meal/weekly planners, and lined notebooks are widely and repeatedly described by KDP-focused publishers themselves as saturated to the point of being avoided by experienced low-content authors. Confidence: high (multiple independent sources agree).
- **[02-durability]** ADHD-specific and other condition-specific planning ("body double" timers, executive-function trackers) look durable because they map to a persistent, underserved buyer need rather than a stylistic trend, even though the exact keyword/format may shift. Confidence: med.
- **[02-durability]** Handwriting practice and other genuinely educational workbooks (as opposed to entertainment activity books) tend to be more durable because purchases are tied to parenting/schooling needs rather than novelty. Confidence: med (reasoning-based, not a dedicated source).
- **[02-durability]** Teen/tween mental-health-adjacent workbooks and confidence/friendship journals look durable, riding a multi-year rise in youth-mental-health awareness among parents and schools, distinct from short-lived teen aesthetic micro-trends (e.g., specific coloring aesthetics). Confidence: med.

### [03-seasonality] Seasonal / gift-driven spikes and lead time

- **[03-seasonality]** Holiday gift season (roughly Black Friday through Christmas) is the single biggest spike window for puzzle books, coloring books, and planners aimed at adults and seniors; publishers typically need titles live and reviewed well before mid-November, implying a design/publish lead time of at least 2–3 months. Confidence: high (general publishing-industry pattern; specific lead times `unverified`).
- **[03-seasonality]** New-year planner and habit-tracker demand spikes around January 1 (resolution season); Etsy digital planner listings visibly reference the upcoming year (2026–2027, 2027) well in advance, implying sellers publish 3–6 months ahead of the calendar year. Confidence: med.
- **[03-seasonality]** Back-to-school (roughly July–September in the US) drives handwriting practice, homeschool worksheets, and kids' activity/educational books; lead time should target availability by early-to-mid summer. Confidence: med (reasoning from category logic; not independently confirmed with sales data).
- **[03-seasonality]** Mother's Day and Father's Day create secondary spikes for adult coloring books, mindfulness journals, and puzzle books positioned as gifts — smaller than the winter holidays but recurring annually. Confidence: med.
- **[03-seasonality]** Grandparent-gift occasions (birthdays, "just because" gifts to seniors) appear to sustain large-print puzzle book demand somewhat independent of the big holiday calendar, based on how consistently "gift for grandma/grandpa" framing appears across senior puzzle book titles and Etsy reviews. Confidence: low-med.
- **[03-seasonality]** Single-use seasonal coloring/activity books (a specific holiday theme with no year-round use) are flagged by a 2026 trend source as a declining format relative to more evergreen themed books — implying seasonal titles alone are a riskier bet than themed-but-year-round books. Confidence: med.

### [04-audience-pull] What each audience responds to

- **[04-audience-pull]** Senior Large Print: readability is treated as a primary buying factor — large clear grids, one puzzle per page or two-per-page spacious layouts, minimal visual clutter, and "familiar rules, no obscure words" framing recur across product copy and buying-guide language. Confidence: high.
- **[04-audience-pull]** Senior Large Print: nostalgia theming (specific decades, "throwback," historical trivia, faith content) consistently outperforms unthemed puzzle books in how sellers position their products, because the theme becomes the searchable differentiator. Confidence: med-high.
- **[04-audience-pull]** Adult: wellness/mindfulness framing (stress relief, anxiety, mental clarity) is layered onto almost every adult puzzle and coloring category examined — cryptograms, word search, and coloring books are all marketed with mindfulness language rather than as pure entertainment. Confidence: high.
- **[04-audience-pull]** Adult (Etsy specifically): practicality and customization are the two most cited purchase drivers — editable Canva templates, hyperlinked digital planners for GoodNotes/Notability, and niche-specific trackers (ADHD, pets, self-care) outperform generic one-size-fits-all planners. Confidence: med-high.
- **[04-audience-pull]** Kids 4-8: familiar, friendly, simple subjects (farm animals, dinosaurs, vehicles) with large, forgiving artwork and short activities that don't require sustained attention span. Confidence: high (consistent across many kids' product listings observed).
- **[04-audience-pull]** Kids 6-10 / Tween 8-12: increasing appetite for challenge and mastery (progressive difficulty mazes/logic puzzles, "smart kids" branding) and multi-activity variety books that hold attention longer than single-format books. Confidence: med.
- **[04-audience-pull]** Teen 13-17: aesthetic identity and social-media-driven micro-trends (studygram, dark academia, cottagecore) strongly shape what sells in coloring and planner printables; mental-health-aware framing (anxiety, stress, self-care) also resonates but is a distinct, more durable pull than pure aesthetics. Confidence: med.

### [05-gaps] Under-served angles

- **[05-gaps]** Spot-the-difference books are explicitly called out by a KDP niche researcher as a real gap: demand exists, but the interior artwork is harder and more expensive to produce well, keeping supply thin relative to buyer interest. Confidence: med.
- **[05-gaps]** Themed/niche word search (profession-specific, hobby-specific, faith-specific, state/region-specific) consistently outperforms generic word search because the theme functions as a long-tail keyword; many publishers still default to unthemed books, leaving room in narrower themes. Confidence: med-high.
- **[05-gaps]** Condition- or need-specific planners and trackers (ADHD, pet care, chronic illness/health logs) are named as under-saturated relative to generic productivity planners on Etsy. Confidence: med.
- **[05-gaps]** Youth mental-health/confidence journals for tweens sit in a gap between saturated adult gratitude journals and a smaller but growing kids'-wellness market — less competition than the adult equivalent. Confidence: low-med (reasoning-based; not a dedicated source).
- **[05-gaps]** Grayscale/photo-realistic coloring is explicitly named as less saturated than standard adult coloring line art, representing a stylistic gap within an otherwise crowded category. Confidence: med.
- **[05-gaps]** Multi-format bundles that combine a puzzle/coloring/journal element with a practical tracker (e.g., a pet planner plus a coloring cover, or a mindfulness workbook plus habit tracker) appear less common than single-purpose products, suggesting room for cross-category bundling, though this is an inference rather than a directly sourced finding. Confidence: low.
