**[04-gaps]**

- **Age band:** Tween (8–12/10–14) and, to a lesser extent, teen (13–17) are thin across nearly every genre in this list — puzzles, journals, and coloring alike default to "kids" or "adult" framing with little built specifically for the in-between reader.
- **Accessibility:** Large print is repeatedly called underserved _outside_ of puzzles — i.e., large-print planners, bullet journals, and reflective journals for seniors are much rarer than large-print sudoku/word search, even though the same buyer plausibly wants both.
- **Format:** Cryptograms, KenKen/Kakuro, and nonograms remain structurally under-published relative to their demand because they require more sophisticated generation than randomized word-search grids — a durable (not just seasonal) technical moat for anyone who invests in proper puzzle-generation tooling.
- **Coloring style:** Grayscale/photo-realistic coloring and "bold and easy" thick-outline coloring are both described as currently under-supplied relative to demand, versus a glutted, declining generic-mandala/zentangle middle.
- **Audience specificity in journals/trackers:** Caregiver-specific and neurodivergent-specific (ADHD) planning/tracking products are named specifically as underserved relative to generic gratitude/habit-tracker content, which is itself description as commoditized.
- **Profession and micro-community theming:** Nurses, teachers, truckers, gardeners, and similaverage affinity groups are repeatedly cited as an outperforming angle for puzzle books, and the same logic is transferable to journals and planners, which mostly haven't picked it up yet outside a few examples.

**[Q5-positioning]**

Rather than competing head-on in the largest, most commoditized cells (generic adult sudoku, generic mandala coloring, generic gratitude journals — all flagged above as saturated with weak differentiation available), the set should concentrate on **compounding two under-served slices at once**: a narrow age band (tween/teen) or accessibility need (large print) _combined with_ an under-published format or audience angle (cryptograms/logic puzzles, grayscale coloring, caregiver- or neurodivergent-specific tracking, profession theming). Single-factor differentiation (just "themed," or just "large print") is common enough that it's a starting hygiene factor, not a moat; the durable gaps found here are the intersections — e.g., cryptograms _for teens_, large-print _bullet journals_ for seniors, caregiver-specific _coping journals_ rather than generic gratitude journals. Positioning line: _"Puzzle, coloring, and journal products for the readers the big publishers' age bands and accessibility defaults leave out — built at real, verified production quality rather than templated volume."_
