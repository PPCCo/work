## PART B

### [Q1-title-patterns]

**Puzzle books** — `[Optional format qualifier] + [Puzzle Type] + for + [Audience] + : + [Count] + [Difficulty] + Puzzles [+ format feature]`

- "Sudoku Puzzles for Adults: 200 Easy to Hard Puzzles"
- "Large Print Crossword Puzzles for Seniors: 100 Easy Puzzles with Solutions"
- "My First Word Search for Kids: 50 Simple Puzzles, Ages 6-10"
  Keep the on-cover title itself short (roughly 3-8 words); push count/difficulty/audience detail into the subtitle field, since Amazon counts title + subtitle together (see length norms below).

**Coloring books** — `[Theme] + Coloring Book + for + [Audience] + : + [Count] + [Descriptor] + Designs/Pages`

- "Mandala Coloring Book for Adults: 100 Intricate Designs, Single-Sided Pages"
- "Animal Coloring Book for Kids: 50 Simple Designs, Ages 4-8"
  Describe the pages (single-sided, page count, trim size) rather than the feeling they're supposed to produce — "single-sided, easy to frame" is a fact; "stress-relieving" or "calming" is a wellness claim you can't verify per book, so it's safer left out or used only as a very soft, non-medical aside if you choose to keep it at all.

**Journals / planners / trackers / workbooks** — `[Type] + Journal/Planner + for + [Audience] + : + [Structure or Duration], [Feature]`

- "Daily Gratitude Journal for Adults: 90 Days of Guided Prompts, Undated"
- "Habit Tracker Journal for Adults: 12-Month Undated Tracker with Monthly Reviews"
  Length norms across both channels tend to reward front-loading the primary keyword in the first 40-50 characters (that's what shows in thumbnails/mobile search), then using the remaining subtitle/tag space for audience and feature terms rather than repeating the same word.

### [Q3-subtitle]

Confirmed current platform limits (checked against Amazon's and Etsy's own help pages plus several 2026 seller guides — reconfirm in your dashboard, since these can change):

- **Amazon KDP:** title + subtitle combined must be **200 characters or fewer**; up to **7 backend keyword fields, 50 characters each**; up to **3 browse categories** per format.
- **Etsy:** listing title up to **140 characters**; up to **13 tags, 20 characters each**.

Truthful differentiators to carry in the subtitle/tags, and how to phrase them honestly:

- **Count** — state the actual number of puzzles/pages/designs you will ship. Don't round up.
- **Difficulty** — name the tier(s) truthfully (Easy / Medium / Hard, or a stated range). Don't label a book "Easy" if the puzzles inside are hard.
- **Large print** — only use this word if the interior type is genuinely enlarged (commonly 16pt+ in this niche). Don't apply the label to a standard-size interior just because the audience is older.
- **Physical format facts** — single-sided pages, solutions included/not included, spiral-bound (only if actually spiral-bound), trim size.
- **Gift angle** — describe the audience/occasion factually ("for puzzle-loving grandparents," "for road trips") instead of asserting it's "the perfect gift," "guaranteed to delight," or similar unverifiable superlatives.
- **Wellness/mindfulness angle** — describe the activity itself (guided prompts, reflection questions, coloring pages) rather than promising an effect (calm, healing, stress relief) you can't verify per buyer.

### [Q4-series-naming]

- Pick **one** convention per set and keep it identical across every volume: either sequential ("Book 1," "Book 2," "Book 3") or tiered by difficulty ("Easy," "Medium," "Hard"). Don't mix the two within one set.
- Keep the **set name word-for-word identical** across volumes; only the differentiator (number or tier) changes. This is what makes browsers read them as a family and is also what lets a reader search your author page for "more like this."
- Match the **subtitle template** across volumes too — same structure, same feature callouts, only the count/difficulty/volume number swapped.
- Register the set in **KDP's Series field** (and, informally, in your Etsy shop section naming) so the platform itself links the volumes for readers.
- Keep cover typography and color-coding consistent per set (e.g., a color per difficulty tier) so the family reads visually, not just textually.

### [Q5-avoid]

- **Trademarks / branded terms:** don't use "Wordle," "NYT" or "New York Times," "Highlights," Disney/Marvel/Pixar/Nintendo or any character names, sports league names, or any other branded IP in a title, subtitle, or keyword. Generic puzzle-type words (Sudoku, Crossword, Kakuro, Nonogram) are not trademarks and are fine to use freely. Also avoid naming specific trivia-show brands in a trivia book's title or keywords.
- **Medical / therapeutic claims:** no "reduces anxiety," "treats," "therapy," "clinically proven," "cures," "heals," or diagnostic language anywhere near a mindfulness, coloring, or journaling product. Describe these as creative or reflective activities, not medical interventions.
- **Misleading superlatives / unverifiable claims:** "best," "world's greatest," "#1 bestseller" (unless it is genuinely and currently true — and even then it's a temporary, checkable claim, not something to bake permanently into a title), "guaranteed," invented awards, or fabricated review/rating counts.
- **Keyword stuffing:** Amazon explicitly reviews for and can reject titles padded with repeated keywords or promotional phrases; Etsy's algorithm also treats repeated words as wasted space. Say it once, cleanly.
- **Repeating title words in your backend keywords/tags:** both platforms already index your title and subtitle, so re-typing those same words into your 7 KDP keyword fields or 13 Etsy tags wastes that space — use it for synonyms and phrases you haven't used yet.
