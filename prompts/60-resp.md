## PART B

**[Q1-trim] Common trim sizes by product + audience**

Puzzle and activity books (all ages) converge overwhelmingly on **8.5x11 (US Letter)**: this is the most commonly recommended size because the larger format gives puzzles room to breathe and makes them easier to use, which matters especially for large-print books where readability is everything. One formatting guide put a number on it: 92 percent of top-selling puzzle books on Amazon use 8.5 x 11 inches, because standard grids (a 20x20 word search, a sudoku grid at 6.5x6.5 inches) simply don't fit comfortably in a smaller trim. A 6x9 "pocket/travel" variant shows up as a secondary option for text-lighter puzzle types (cryptograms, trivia) or commuter-focused books.

Coloring books split by audience: 8.5x11 is the most popular format for adult coloring books, since the extra space supports higher-detail artwork and gives colorists room to work, while 8.5x8.5 square is common for children's books and simpler bold-and-easy styles. For adults, 8.5x11 is the default trim, with 8.5x8.5 reserved for bold-and-easy, children's, and gift-style coloring books.

Guided journals and planners lean smaller than puzzle/coloring formats: 6x9 inches is the go-to trim size for most journal publishers, since it's comfortable to write in and keeps printing costs reasonable, and a dedicated journal-size guide confirms 6x9 and 8.5x11 are the two common winners, with size affecting cost, reader expectations, and cover design. In practice this splits by sub-type: a guided journal with prompts and themed pages (gratitude, prayer, fitness, dream) typically runs 100-120 pages, while a planner organizing daily/weekly/monthly time typically runs 120-200 pages and is usually built at 8.5x11 to fit the extra grid space each day/week needs.

**[Q2-page-band] Typical page-count bands**

- Puzzle books: low ~50 pages (single-difficulty, single-type minis) / typical ~100-200 pages / high 250+ (compilation/"1000+ puzzle" volumes seen in bestseller listings).
- Coloring books (single-sided, the norm for adults): most successful coloring books run 50-70 designs with blank backs, totaling 100-140 pages, with a 24-page KDP minimum; a second source puts the sweet spot slightly lower at 80-100 pages (40-50 designs plus blank backs for bleed protection). Treat 80-140 pages as the realistic band.
- Guided journals: typical 100-120 pages for prompted/guided journals; planners typical 120-200 pages because each day or week occupies multiple pages.
- Digital printables: no fixed "page count" convention — sold as packs/bundles, commonly 1-50 pages per listing depending on whether it's a single design or a full planner system.

**[Q3-print-vs-digital] Format choice and norms**

- **Paperback** is the dominant format across puzzle books, coloring books, and journals on KDP-style self-publishing — it's the default assumed by essentially every production guide referenced above.
- **Hardcover** is a smaller niche, mostly for premium gift-style journals/planners; KDP hardcover is available in five sizes (5.5x8.5, 6x9, 6.14x9.21, 7x10, and 8.25x11), so hardcover is a realistic upsell for a flagship journal SKU but rarely used for puzzle or coloring interiors.
- **Digital PDF** is the format for printables sold on marketplaces like Etsy, plus increasingly for "printable inserts" versions of journals/planners as a lower-price, no-shipping companion product to the paperback.
- Interior color: black & white/grayscale interiors are standard for puzzle books, coloring books, and most journals to control print cost; color interiors are reserved for niches like cookbooks or premium journals where the cost is justified.

**[Q4-printable] Digital printable page size and DPI**

The overwhelming majority of printable buyers fall into two camps — A4 (most of the world) or US Letter (primarily the US and Canada) — so covering both sizes captures nearly all buyers without needing a long tail of extra formats. Exact specs: US Letter at 300 DPI is 2550x3300 px and A4 at 300 DPI is 2480x3508 px (A5 and Half Letter are common third/fourth options for planner-style products). General guidance across listings is consistent: 300 DPI PDF ensures crisp prints, sized for A4, Letter, A5, and Half Letter, with instructions to print at 100%/actual size rather than "fit to page". A practical safe-zone note: most home printers leave a 5-10mm unprintable border, so keeping a 10mm safe zone on all sides and labeling the listing "print at actual size" avoids cropped text.

**[Q5-binding/paper] Paper and binding hints**

- **Single-sided is the strong default for adult coloring books.** Standard KDP paper is 55 lb (90 GSM) and is not marker-proof — alcohol markers and gel pens will bleed through to the back — so the reliable fix is single-sided design with the illustration on one side and the back left blank.
- **Double-sided is fine for kids' coloring books** since kids primarily use crayons and colored pencils, which don't bleed, and double-sided cuts print cost.
- **Bleed and margins for coloring interiors:** 0.125 inches of bleed on all sides if art touches the edge, 0.375" minimum outside margin with bleed (0.25" without), and a 0.375" inside gutter for 24-150 pages, stepping up at higher page counts.
- **Puzzle book margins:** one spec sheet recommends a 0.75" gutter, 0.625" outside margin, and 0.75" top/bottom on an 8.5x11 page — tighter than KDP's bare minimum but giving a "premium feel" and enough room for a puzzle grid, title, and word list.
- **Lay-flat or spiral binding** is worth flagging as a premium option for puzzle books and coloring books people write/color directly into, though standard perfect-bound paperback remains the default and cheaper baseline.
- General page-count mechanic worth remembering for any format decision: KDP requires an even page count and margins that scale with page count (roughly 0.375"-0.875" inside margin depending on total pages), which affects how tight you can run a short single-topic puzzle or coloring volume.
